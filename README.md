[How To Be A Better Reader (Do This To Your Books) by Daniel Pink](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vhZ6QAYlA_g&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Summary

In this video Daniel Pink explains **seven practical techniques** that help you read more effectively and retain what you read. The focus is not on speed, but on **active engagement with the material** so you internalize ideas rather than skim them. The first key idea is to treat your book like a partner in learning, not a sacred object to protect. This means physically and mentally interacting with it: crack the spine, underline important passages, dog-ear pages, and make notes in the margins. This engages your brain and deepens comprehension. 

Next, Pink introduces a method he calls **Summarize + Harvest (S & H)**. After each chapter, write a few sentences in your own words about the main point. At the end of the book, ask yourself three questions: *What is the big idea? How does the author support it? What should I do with this insight?* This not only reinforces memory but turns reading into action. 

Another technique is to **build a “second brain”** for your reading: keep your summaries, highlights and notes in a digital space like Notion or Docs. You can then use these organized notes for future reference or even feed them into AI to extract quotes or themes. 

Pink also emphasizes **rereading strategically**. A great book can teach you different things at different stages of your life because *you* change. He then encourages becoming a **T-shaped reader**, meaning you go deep in subjects you care about while also reading broadly across genres so ideas from one area can enrich another. 

He talks about knowing **when to quit** a book too: if it isn’t engaging you after a reasonable amount of reading, it’s okay to set it aside. The idea is that a book should *earn* your attention, not be something you struggle through out of guilt. Use this formular: 100 pages minus your age. If still boring and awful then quit the book.

Finally, Pink reminds us not to treat reading as a chore or performance metric, but as a **joyful and intentional practice**. He encourages readers to find pleasure in the process and not stress about finishing everything on their list.
